        Inaccessible PDFs are now a compliance problem

        Accessibility rules that cover documents are already in force or about to be. Most organisations still publish untagged PDFs.

        • Since 28 June 2025

          EU Accessibility Act

          Applies to e‑commerce, banking, e‑books, transport and telecom services sold in the EU. The harmonised standard EN 301 549 includes requirements for documents.

        • 26 April 2027 · 2028

          US ADA Title II web rule

          State and local governments must meet WCAG 2.1 AA: by 26 April 2027 for populations of 50,000 or more, and by 26 April 2028 for smaller entities and special districts. Documents are in scope.

        • Today

          People who need it

          Screen reader users, people who zoom to read, people with dyslexia, and anyone reading a council agenda on a phone. An untagged PDF fails every one of them.

        Sources: EU Directive 2019/882 and the US Department of Justice ADA Title II web accessibility rule (28 CFR 35.200). This is not legal advice.

        How AltPass works

        1. 1

          Read the layout

          pdf.js extracts text with positions, font sizes, images and links. Headings come from size and weight, columns from gutters, tables from aligned cells.

        2. 2

          Rebuild and describe

          The structure becomes semantic HTML. Each image gets alt text from a vision model when a key is configured, otherwise from its caption and context, always flagged for review.

        3. 3

          Prove it

          Weighted checks score the PDF before and the HTML after. axe‑core runs on both, live in your browser, including colour contrast.

        What AltPass cannot do (yet)

        • Structure is inferred. Headings, lists, tables and reading order are heuristics from the page layout. Always skim the outline.
        • Alt text is a draft. Without a vision‑model key it is written from the caption and nearby text, not by looking at the picture. Every draft is flagged for a person to review.
        • Automated checks are partial. axe‑core only finds what a machine can detect. A clean run is necessary, not sufficient. This is not a certification.
        • No scans. PDFs without selectable text need OCR, which is not included. Forms, maths and complex tables are out of scope.
